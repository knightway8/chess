What an opening should accomplish
=================================

The opening should produce a playable middlegame while avoiding immediate tactical damage.
Control useful central space, develop pieces toward real jobs, safeguard the king, and
coordinate the rooks. These aims interact; a central pawn move may gain space and free a
bishop, while a developing move may also defend the center.

Do not count development by pieces moved. A knight developed to a square where a pawn
immediately drives it away has not saved time. A queen sortie that wins a pawn but gives the
opponent several useful developing attacks may fall behind. Judge whether each move improves
access and coordination.

Central control can use occupation, pressure, or a planned challenge. Classical openings
often place pawns in the center. Hypermodern openings may invite a central pawn mass, then
attack it with pieces and pawn breaks. Neither method excuses passivity. The occupied center
must be supported; the invited center must be challenged.

King safety usually favors early castling, but determine where the king will be safer. An
already damaged wing may be a poor destination. A closed center can occasionally permit
delayed castling. If the center can open through a forcing break, leaving the king there
becomes dangerous.

A move can violate a general principle for a concrete reason. Moving a piece twice may win
material, prevent a threat, or reach an essential outpost. Taking a wing pawn with the queen
may be safe in a specific line. State the reason and calculate the response. 'Grandmasters
do it' is not the reason.

The opening ends at different times in different games. One player may still be solving
development while the other begins a direct attack. Use the position's readiness rather than
a fixed move number. Before planning an attack, check whether your least active piece and
king are ready.

Exercise: after ten individual turns by each side in one game, assign every piece a job.
Identify the move that did the least for development, center, king safety, or coordination.
Replace it with a move that serves at least two purposes.
