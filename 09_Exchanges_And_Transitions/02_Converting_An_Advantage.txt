Converting an advantage
=======================

Conversion means turning an advantage into a result while controlling the opponent's
counterplay. First identify the nature of the advantage. Extra material suggests exchanges
of active enemy pieces. More space suggests improving pieces and creating another front. A
safer king suggests opening lines before the defender consolidates.

Do not hurry when the advantage is stable. Improve your worst piece, restrict the opponent's
break, and make an escape square before taking a pawn. Do act when the advantage is
temporary. A development lead may disappear, and an exposed king may find safety. The source
of the advantage determines its clock.

Choose favorable transformations. An extra pawn may be easiest to use in a rook ending,
hardest in an opposite-colored bishop ending, or decisive in a pawn ending. These are
tendencies; exact placement can reverse them. Calculate the destination before trading
toward it.

Reduce the defender's active ideas. If perpetual check is the only danger, secure the king
or trade queens. If a passed pawn is the only danger, blockade it before collecting
material. Leaving one form of counterplay alive can be enough to lose control.

Avoid the desire to prove superiority with the fastest win. A clear path that takes longer
is still clear. Under time pressure, prefer positions with safe plans and fewer tactical
reversals, provided the resulting ending is actually favorable.

Exercise: at a moment when you were better, list the opponent's three drawing or swindling
resources. Did your move remove one, ignore one, or create a new one? Conversion skill grows
from treating resistance as part of the position.
