Queen's Gambit, Slav, Semi-Slav, and Tarrasch families
======================================================

QUEEN'S GAMBIT

White offers a wing pawn to distract Black's central pawn and build a stronger center. This
is usually a positional gambit: Black cannot safely keep the extra pawn without concessions
in many lines. The central structure matters more than the temporary material offer.

QUEEN'S GAMBIT DECLINED

Black supports the center and maintains tension. White develops and seeks pressure on the
center, while Black aims for freeing breaks and queen-bishop activity. Orthodox structures
can lead to a minority attack on the queenside. The Exchange Variation fixes the pawn
structure early and makes plans clearer without removing tactical details.

QUEEN'S GAMBIT ACCEPTED

Black releases central tension and takes the offered pawn, usually intending development
rather than permanent pawn defense. White builds a center and recovers the pawn or gains
activity. Black challenges the center and seeks a free game. Clinging to the pawn can cost
too many tempi.

SLAV DEFENSE

The Slav supports the center with the queen-bishop pawn and often preserves a route for the
queen bishop. Black obtains a sound structure. White chooses between central pressure, quiet
development, and sharp attempts to exploit the bishop's movement away from the queenside.

SEMI-SLAV DEFENSE

The Semi-Slav combines the central support of the Queen's Gambit Declined and Slav but
temporarily blocks the queen bishop. It prepares central expansion and can lead to some of
the sharpest queen-pawn positions. Meran structures emphasize central breaks and active
bishops; Botvinnik-type systems can become deeply tactical with material imbalance.

TARRASCH DEFENSE

The Tarrasch strikes the center actively and often accepts an isolated central pawn for free
piece play. Black values activity and open lines; White seeks blockade and favorable
exchanges. The Semi-Tarrasch changes the exchange order and may avoid or transform the
isolated-pawn structure.

ALBIN COUNTERGAMBIT AND CHIGORIN DEFENSE

The Albin offers a pawn to establish an advanced central pawn and gain activity. White
should blockade and complete development without falling for promotion traps. The Chigorin
develops the queen knight in a way that blocks a pawn but increases piece pressure on the
center. Both challenge quiet Queen's Gambit expectations with concrete play.

Across the family, learn the central breaks, queen-bishop problem, isolated-pawn plans, and
minority attack. Those ideas connect many named variations.
