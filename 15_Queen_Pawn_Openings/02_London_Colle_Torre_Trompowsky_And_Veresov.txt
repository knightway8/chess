London, Colle, Torre, Trompowsky, and Veresov systems
=====================================================

LONDON SYSTEM

The London develops the queen bishop outside the pawn chain and builds a compact central
structure. White obtains reliable development and often attacks the king or uses a central
break. The setup is not independent of Black's choices. Black can challenge the center, seek
an active bishop, or expand on the queenside.

COLLE SYSTEM

The Colle supports the center with pawns, develops the king bishop behind the chain, and
prepares a central break. The break is the point of the setup; without it the bishop remains
restricted. Black seeks space and may develop the queen bishop before the center closes.

TORRE ATTACK

The Torre develops a bishop to pin the king knight and supports a solid center. White may
combine central pressure with a kingside attack. Black can challenge the bishop and seek
central activity. The pin has value only when it affects a real tactical or structural
decision.

TROMPOWSKY ATTACK

The Trompowsky develops the queen bishop aggressively against the king knight, sometimes
exchanging to damage pawns or avoid Indian-defense theory. White gives up the bishop pair in
some lines for structure and development. Black can challenge the center and use the
bishops.

RICHTER-VERESOV ATTACK

The Veresov develops the queen knight behind the queen pawn and brings the bishop out
actively, preparing a central advance. White seeks rapid development and pressure; the
knight can obstruct the usual supporting pawn. Black challenges the center and avoids
passive defense.

RAPPORT-JOBAVA SYSTEM

This system combines the London bishop development with early queen-knight activity,
creating direct threats and unusual piece pressure. It can surprise opponents but does not
remove strategic duties. White should understand when the knight placement supports attack
and when it obstructs the center.

BLACKMAR-DIEMER GAMBIT

White offers a central pawn for development and attacking lines. Black can accept and
consolidate or decline into other structures. White must use time energetically. As with all
gambits, separate sound compensation from traps that depend on inaccurate defense.

SYSTEM LESSON

Systems reduce the number of early choices a learner must memorize, but the opponent's pawn
structure still determines the best plan. Treat the recurring setup as a starting language.
Learn the main break, the bad piece, and the circumstances that require a different
arrangement.
