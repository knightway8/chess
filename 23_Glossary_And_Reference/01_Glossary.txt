Chess concepts glossary
=======================

These definitions are starting points. Their practical meaning depends on the complete
position.

ACTIVITY
The ability of a piece or army to perform useful work: attack targets, control routes,
support breaks, or restrict the opponent.

BACKWARD PAWN
A pawn that trails neighboring pawns and cannot advance safely, often leaving the square in
front of it available to an enemy piece.

BATTERY
Two or more pieces aligned so that their combined pressure operates along the same line or
toward the same target.

BIND
A form of restriction that makes an opponent’s freeing moves difficult without necessarily
winning material immediately.

BLOCKADE
Stopping a pawn, especially a passed or isolated pawn, with a piece placed in front of it.

BREAK
A pawn advance intended to challenge another pawn and change the structure or open lines.

CANDIDATE MOVE
A serious possible move selected for comparison and calculation.

CLEARANCE
Vacating a line or square so another piece can use it.

COMPENSATION
Activity, structure, king pressure, passers, or other benefits received in return for
material.

CONNECTED PASSED PAWNS
Passed pawns on adjacent files that can support one another as they advance.

CONVERSION
Turning an advantage into a durable gain or final result while controlling counterplay.

COUNTERPLAY
Active possibilities that give the disadvantaged or defending side threats of its own.

DECOY
A tactic that draws a piece to a square where another idea becomes possible.

DEFLECTION
A tactic that pulls a defender away from its duty.

DEVELOPMENT
Bringing pieces into useful play with access, safety, and purpose.

DISCOVERED ATTACK
An attack revealed when a blocking piece moves away from a line piece.

DOUBLED PAWNS
Friendly pawns occupying the same file.

EXCHANGE
A trade of material; “the exchange” also commonly means the material difference between a
rook and a minor piece.

FIANCHETTO
Developing a bishop on a long diagonal behind a one-step knight-pawn advance.

FILE
A vertical column of eight squares.

FLIGHT SQUARE
A legal square available for a king to escape checks or a mating net.

FORK
A single move or piece attack against two or more targets.

FORTRESS
A stable defensive setup that the materially stronger side cannot successfully penetrate or
transform.

GAMBIT
A voluntary offer of material for time, activity, structure, or an attack.

HALF-OPEN FILE
A file with no pawn of one side but at least one opposing pawn.

HANGING PAWNS
Two connected pawns on adjacent files without friendly pawns beside them.

INITIATIVE
The ability to make threats that force the opponent to respond.

INTERFERENCE
Blocking a line between a defender and what it protects.

ISOLATED PAWN
A pawn with no friendly pawn on either adjacent file.

LEVER
A pawn advance that creates direct pawn contact and the possibility of an exchange.

LOOSE PIECE
A piece that is undefended or inadequately defended for the tactics in the position.

MAJORITY
More pawns than the opponent in one region of the board.

MINOR PIECE
A bishop or knight.

MINORITY ATTACK
Advancing a smaller pawn group against a larger group to provoke a fixed weakness.

OPEN FILE
A file containing no pawns.

OPPOSITION
A king relationship in which the inability of kings to stand adjacent can force one king to
yield access.

OUTPOST
A useful, protected square where a piece cannot conveniently be driven away by an enemy
pawn.

OVERLOADING
Giving a defender incompatible duties that cannot all be met.

OVERPROTECTION
Providing extra support to a strategically important point so the supporting pieces remain
coordinated and flexible.

PASSED PAWN
A pawn with no opposing pawn on its file or neighboring files able to stop it through normal
pawn contact.

PIN
A restriction on a piece whose movement would expose a king or valuable target behind it.

PLY
One individual turn by one player; two plies make one complete move by both players.

PROPHYLAXIS
Anticipating and limiting the opponent’s useful plans and resources.

RANK
A horizontal row of eight squares.

SACRIFICE
A deliberate material investment for a concrete or positional benefit.

SKEWER
A line attack in which an urgent front target moves and exposes another target behind it.

SPACE
Useful room for pieces, usually created through pawn control of territory.

TEMPO
One turn of play, often discussed as time gained or lost in executing a plan.

TRANSPOSITION
Reaching the same complete position through a different move order.

UNDERPROMOTION
Promoting a pawn to a rook, bishop, or knight instead of a queen.

X-RAY
Pressure exerted along a line through an intervening piece.

ZUGZWANG
A position in which the obligation to move worsens the player’s position.
