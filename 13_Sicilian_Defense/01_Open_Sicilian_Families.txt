The Open Sicilian: Najdorf, Dragon, Classical, and Scheveningen
===============================================================

The Sicilian creates an asymmetrical center from the first exchange. Black trades a flank
pawn for White's central pawn in many main lines, gaining a half-open file and a central
pawn majority. White gains space and rapid development. Both sides accept imbalance instead
of immediate symmetry.

NAJDORF

The Najdorf uses a flexible queenside pawn move to prevent a bishop check and prepare
expansion. Black delays some commitments while White chooses among direct king attacks,
positional bishop development, and quieter systems. The central break is essential;
queenside activity alone can be too slow. White must match an attack with development and
watch Black's counterplay.

DRAGON

The Dragon develops the king bishop on a long diagonal and pressures the center and
queenside. White often builds an attack with opposite-wing castling, while Black attacks on
the open file and diagonal. The race is concrete. Removing the long-diagonal bishop can
weaken Black's king, but the exchange costs time and may open lines for Black.

ACCELERATED DRAGON

The Accelerated Dragon seeks the central freeing advance in one move by delaying a
supporting pawn move. White may establish a space bind that restricts the break. Black
attacks the bind with pieces and pawn levers. The move order changes which attacking systems
are available, so do not transfer Dragon tactics automatically.

CLASSICAL SICILIAN

The Classical develops both knights naturally and keeps several structures available. White
can launch a sharp bishop-led attack or choose quieter development. Black seeks central
counterplay and piece activity. Because the setup is less committed, both players must
recognize transpositions.

SCHEVENINGEN

The Scheveningen uses a compact central pawn formation controlling key squares while
preserving two central breaks. White has more space and may attack the king. Black's
structure is resilient if the freeing breaks arrive. Passive waiting is dangerous;
counterplay is part of the defense.

SVESHNIKOV AND KALASHNIKOV

These families accept a backward central pawn and weakened squares in exchange for activity,
space, and dynamic piece play. Black's advanced pawn gains territory but leaves a stable
outpost. White seeks to use the long-term squares; Black seeks time and breaks before those
weaknesses become decisive.

Across all Open Sicilians, learn the pawn breaks, open-file ownership, and king placements
before memorizing forcing lines. Similar-looking attacks can differ because one supporting
pawn or defender has changed.
