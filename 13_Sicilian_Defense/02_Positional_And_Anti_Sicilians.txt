Taimanov, Kan, Closed Sicilian, and Anti-Sicilians
==================================================

TAIMANOV

The Taimanov develops the queen knight and supports a flexible central setup, often with an
early queen move that keeps bishop options open. Black aims for central and queenside
breaks. White can build a broad center or choose direct development. Move-order tactics
matter because Black delays the king knight.

KAN

The Kan uses a modest queenside pawn move and central support to stay flexible. Black may
develop the queen bishop actively and choose between several central structures. The price
is slower kingside development, so White can gain space. White should not overextend merely
because Black's setup looks passive.

FOUR KNIGHTS AND PIN VARIATIONS

The Sicilian Four Knights emphasizes rapid minor-piece development and central pressure. Pin
variations create immediate tactical tension around the king knight. Both require exact
board study because an early bishop or queen move changes recapture options and central
breaks.

O'KELLY

The O'Kelly inserts a queenside pawn move before the usual central exchange. It can
discourage some standard White setups while making others more effective because Black has
spent a tempo away from development. White should choose a structure that exploits the exact
order rather than forcing a favorite line.

ALAPIN

The Alapin supports a broad pawn center before developing the queen knight. Black challenges
that center immediately with a pawn break or pressure. White may accept an isolated central
pawn for activity. The resulting positions connect opening study with isolated-pawn
strategy.

CLOSED SICILIAN

White keeps the center closed and develops the queen knight behind a pawn, often preparing a
kingside pawn advance. Black expands on the queenside and challenges the center. Timing
decides whether White's attack arrives before Black opens lines.

GRAND PRIX ATTACK

White advances the king-bishop pawn and develops for a direct kingside attack. The advance
gains space but weakens squares and can restrict a knight. Black seeks central counterplay
and may place pieces to blunt the attacking bishop. A quick attack without enough pieces can
leave lasting weaknesses.

ROSSOLIMO AND MOSCOW

White develops a bishop to check or pin a knight, often damaging structure or avoiding Open
Sicilian theory. The exchange can surrender the bishop pair while creating doubled pawns.
White aims to use structure and development; Black seeks bishop activity and central play.

SMITH-MORRA AND WING GAMBITS

The Smith-Morra offers a central pawn for development and open files. The Wing Gambit offers
a side pawn to divert Black's flank pawn. Black can accept but must complete development.
White must show concrete activity; trap value alone is insufficient.

BOWDLER AND QUIET SYSTEMS

Early bishop development toward the king is natural but may allow Black comfortable central
play if unsupported. Quiet systems remain playable when they have a plan for the center.
Against every Anti-Sicilian, Black should identify White's altered structure instead of
mechanically following Open Sicilian development.
