Draw rules that use counting
============================

Some draw rules depend on repeated positions and on stretches without captures or pawn
moves. Competitive procedures matter, so this lesson is an explanation rather than a
substitute for the current event rules and an arbiter.

Under the FIDE Laws effective from January 2023, a player can claim a draw when the same
position is about to appear for at least the third time through an indicated move, or has
appeared at least three times with that player to move. The occurrences do not need to be
consecutive. Position identity includes the player to move and the same possible moves,
including relevant castling and passing-capture rights.

The same rules make a draw automatic after the position appears at least five times. This is
distinct from the claim-based threefold rule. A player should not assume that an interface,
casual club, or other federation uses identical claim procedures.

A player can also make a correct claim based on fifty moves by each player without a pawn
move or capture, including an intended move that completes that span under the specified
procedure. An automatic draw occurs after at least seventy-five moves by each player without
a pawn move or capture, unless the last move checkmates; mate takes precedence in that case.

Notice the unit: 'fifty moves by each player' means one hundred individual turns in the
completed span. The counter resets after a capture or pawn move. Checks do not themselves
reset it.

These rules affect endgame strategy. A theoretically winning construction may need progress
before the automatic limit. A defender may track repetition or the no-capture counter as a
resource. Accurate scoresheets and arbiter procedure matter in over-the-board play.

Source note: verify current wording and procedures in the official FIDE Laws at
https://handbook.fide.com/chapter/e012023, especially the drawn-game article. Event
regulations and online platforms may differ.
