Engine evaluations are measurements with conditions
===================================================

Chess engines display numerical evaluations, often in pawn-like units. Positive and negative
signs indicate which side the interface favors, but conventions can differ. A value near one
does not mean the side is literally guaranteed to win one pawn or has a fixed probability
independent of the engine and settings.

Modern Stockfish normalizes its displayed evaluation using a model connected to expected
winning chances in its self-play conditions. Its documentation explains that one pawn unit
in the normalized scale corresponds to about a fifty-percent win probability for the side to
move under the model's specified engine-versus-engine time control. That calibration is not
a universal forecast for two human players.

Depth, search time, hardware, engine version, and position affect the result. A tactical
score can swing when the engine sees a hidden defense. A stable score across deeper searches
is more reassuring, but it does not explain the plan. Forced mate announcements have a
different meaning from ordinary positional values.

The three outcome estimates sometimes shown by engines are based on model conditions. Human
results can differ because players make different types of mistakes. A position that an
engine holds with precise defense may be extremely difficult for a person. A technically
winning position may remain hard to convert.

Use engines to test claims and find missed resources. First analyze without assistance, then
compare candidates. Ask why the evaluation changed: tactic, king safety, pawn break, or
transition. Copying the first engine move without explanation produces dependence rather
than understanding.

Small numerical differences should be handled cautiously. Two moves with close scores may be
practically equivalent at the search settings. Prefer the move whose resulting plans you
understand unless the engine reveals a concrete defect. Do not build sweeping principles
from a hundredth of a pawn.

Source note: Stockfish's current explanation of normalized values and win-draw-loss modeling
is at https://official-stockfish.github.io/docs/stockfish-wiki/Stockfish-FAQ.html.
Interfaces may present engine output differently.
