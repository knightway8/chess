Endgame triage under time pressure
==================================

When time is short, check forcing facts first: promotion threats, checks, captures, and
whether a king can enter. Count pawn races before pursuing slower plans. One missed tempo
can make every strategic idea irrelevant.

Keep pieces active and connected. A rook giving checks is usually easier to use than one
defending three pawns passively. A king close to the action reduces calculation. Simplify
only into an ending whose result and mechanism you understand.

Use known patterns as compression. Opposition, rook-behind-passer, checking distance, and
bishop-color control reduce the search space. Still verify the assumptions. A blocked route
or checking promotion can invalidate the pattern.

If winning, remove the opponent's main counterplay and avoid unnecessary pawn moves. If
defending, create one concrete problem: a passer, checks, a fork threat, or a stalemate
possibility. The clock increases the value of clear tasks.

Record the position afterward if possible and analyze it slowly. Compare your fast
priorities with the exact requirements. The goal is to improve which features you notice
first, not to criticize yourself for lacking unlimited time.

Exercise: give yourself thirty seconds on a legal endgame position and write only the top
priority. Analyze fully afterward. Track whether your first priority improves across
sessions.
