Queen endings, checks, and shelter
==================================

Queen endings combine passed pawns with repeated checks. An extra pawn can be difficult to
use if the king lacks shelter. A single checking tempo may decide whether a pawn promotes.

Centralize the queen when it can give checks from several angles without being traded or
attacked. Keep the king near shelter. Advancing shelter pawns casually can create new
checking lines.

Perpetual check is a main defensive resource. The stronger side may need to trade queens,
interpose the queen, or give up a pawn to reach safety. Hiding the king often comes before
pushing the passer.

The defender should seek checking distance and attack loose pawns with tempo. Random checks
can drive the enemy king toward safety. Choose checks that preserve access and prevent the
opposing queen from interposing favorably.

Queen trades require calculation of the resulting pawn ending. A trade that ends all checks
may still lose if the defender's king reaches the pawns first. Declining a trade may
preserve drawing checks.

Exercise: before every queen check, predict the king's best destination and your next useful
check. If the sequence leads the king to shelter, choose a different checking pattern or
attack a pawn.
