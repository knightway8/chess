King and pawn fundamentals
==========================

King-and-pawn endings teach opposition, corresponding squares, reserve tempi, breakthroughs,
and promotion races. They require exactness. A one-step difference in king position can
reverse the result.

The kings cannot stand adjacent, so one king can deny the other's advance. Direct opposition
is the simplest form, but spare pawn moves and side routes determine who is forced to yield.
Do not chant 'take the opposition' without calculating the pawn moves that change whose turn
it is.

An outside passed pawn can draw the enemy king away. A protected passer can deny entry and
support the king. A distant majority may be more valuable than a nearby extra pawn because
it creates a second front.

Count a race through promotion and beyond. Determine who promotes first, whether promotion
gives check, and whether the new piece can stop the other passer. Include captures that
alter the route. Visual distance is insufficient.

Zugzwang occurs when having to move harms the player. Preserve reserve pawn moves when they
can pass the turn. Force the enemy king away from a critical defense before pushing the
pawn.

Exercise: verify every claimed win or draw with a legal board or tablebase when the position
has few enough pieces. Memorize the mechanism after understanding it, not as a detached
result.
