Basic checkmating material
==========================

A king and queen can force mate against a bare king; so can a king and rook. The attacking
king must help. The long-range piece restricts the enemy king while its king approaches,
then delivers mate with protection.

Avoid unnecessary checks. Use the queen or rook to reduce the defender's space, bring the
king closer, and preserve at least one legal move until the mating net is ready. Careless
restriction can produce stalemate.

Two bishops with a king can force mate. A bishop and knight with a king can also force mate,
but the technique is difficult and depends on driving the king toward a suitable corner. Two
knights alone cannot force checkmate against a bare king under normal defense, although mate
positions can exist if the defender cooperates or has extra material that changes available
moves.

A single bishop or knight with a king cannot mate a bare king. This does not mean every
position containing only one minor piece besides kings is automatically drawn under every
rules platform; the exact remaining material and possible legal sequences matter. Consult
event rules for adjudication details.

Practice the queen and rook methods until they are reliable without rushing. Learn stalemate
awareness at the same time. Exact board practice is essential because verbal descriptions
cannot specify the final net.

Exercise: start with the defending king in several different areas and complete the mate
using a legal board. Count wasted checks and stalemates, then repeat with fewer unnecessary
moves.
