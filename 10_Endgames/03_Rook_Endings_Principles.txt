Rook ending principles
======================

Rook endings are common and rich in checking resources. Activity often matters more than a
single pawn. A passive rook that merely guards pawns may be unable to check the king or
attack from behind.

Place the rook behind a passed pawn when that keeps it active as the pawn advances. This
applies to friendly and enemy passers, but side checks or an active-rank invasion can
override the guideline. The defending king's shelter and the checking distance decide.

Cutting off the enemy king can be worth a pawn. A rook that prevents the king crossing a
file or rank gives your king time to approach. Count whether the restriction persists after
pawn advances and checks.

The stronger side should shelter the king, coordinate king and rook, and avoid releasing the
defender through careless pawn moves. The defender seeks checking distance, attacks pawns
from the side or rear, and avoids purely passive placement when activity is available.

Known bridge-building and defensive checking positions deserve exact board study. Their
value is not the names but the recurring mechanisms: blocking checks, creating shelter, and
keeping sufficient distance.

Exercise: in every rook ending, compare rook activity before counting pawns. Identify
whether each rook can check, attack a pawn, and return to stop the passer. This prevents
material counting from hiding the real position.
