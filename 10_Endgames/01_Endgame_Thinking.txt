How endgame thinking changes
============================

With fewer pieces, kings become active, passed pawns gain importance, and single tempi
become visible. Endgames are not automatically simple. Reduced material can make exact
calculation more important because there are fewer alternative resources after a mistake.

Start by identifying passed-pawn potential and king routes. Then compare piece activity,
pawn weaknesses, and possible exchanges. A material advantage may be secondary if the
opposing king penetrates first or a rook becomes tied to a passer.

Create a plan with a transformation in mind: activate the king, improve the rook, fix a
pawn, create a passer, or enter a known basic ending. Do not make harmless waiting moves
unless waiting has a purpose. Every pawn move changes future tempi permanently.

When considering an exchange, calculate the pawn ending. A favorable rook ending may become
a lost king-and-pawn ending after trading rooks. Conversely, exchanging can remove the
opponent's only counterplay and make a win routine.

Study exact positions on a board alongside these verbal ideas. Endgame results often depend
on one square or one tempo, which prose without coordinates cannot encode. The role of this
library is to explain what to calculate and why.

Exercise: in each endgame, state the desired placement of your king and most active piece,
then identify what prevents them from getting there. Your plan should address that obstacle.
