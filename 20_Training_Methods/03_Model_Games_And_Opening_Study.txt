Learning from model games
=========================

A model game demonstrates plans in a structure you want to understand. Choose games that
reach the opening through realistic move orders and continue into the middlegame or ending.
Short traps teach one error; long games reveal what happens after sound defense.

Play through the game on a board and stop before critical decisions. Predict a move and
state its purpose. Comparing reasons is more useful than merely checking whether your move
matches.

Study victories for both sides. If your repertoire note contains only wins by your color, it
hides the opponent's best counterplay. Draws can show defensive resources and typical
simplifications.

Extract a small number of reusable facts: main break, best piece, difficult piece, favorable
exchange, common tactical motif, and typical ending. Add these to the verbal opening map.

Do not imitate moves outside their structural context. A wing pawn advance from one model
game may be correct because the center was locked. In another move order the center can open
and punish it.

Revisit the same games. Familiarity lets you notice deeper reasons after the plot is known.
One deeply understood game can teach more than twenty games clicked through quickly.
