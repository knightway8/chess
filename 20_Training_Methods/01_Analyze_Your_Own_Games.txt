How to analyze your own games
=============================

Analyze without an engine first. Record where you felt confused, which candidates you
considered, and what you calculated. These memories disappear quickly, and they reveal
thinking problems that a computer score cannot see.

Identify turning points rather than annotating every move equally. Find the first meaningful
opening deviation, the hardest decision, the first lost opportunity, and the move after
which the result became forced. Separate a worse position from a lost one.

For each turning point, reconstruct your decision: what changed, what was urgent, what
candidates existed, and which reply you missed. Then examine plans and tactics on a legal
board. Only afterward use an engine or stronger reference to test your conclusions.

When the engine disagrees, explain why. Did you miss a capture, misjudge an ending, or
choose the wrong plan? Save the reason as a training theme. Copying the computer move
without the reason produces no reusable lesson.

Review wins as seriously as losses. A win can contain an unsound sacrifice or missed
defense. A loss can contain several excellent decisions before one mistake. Outcome bias
hides both.

Finish with one change to training. If loose pieces caused three errors, solve tactics
around undefended pieces. If time trouble caused rushed decisions, practice shorter
routines. A long list of vague faults creates guilt rather than improvement.
