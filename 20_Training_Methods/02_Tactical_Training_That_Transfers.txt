Tactical training that transfers to games
=========================================

Solve from positions where you do not know a tactic exists. Game-like uncertainty is harder
than a themed set whose title reveals the motif. Use themed drills to learn patterns, then
mixed positions to train recognition and calculation.

Before moving, identify checks, captures, threats, loose pieces, and overloaded defenders.
Calculate the opponent's best response. Do not stop when you see a pleasing first move. A
correct solution includes the reason alternatives fail.

Track error types. Pattern failure means you did not consider the motif. Visualization
failure means you lost track of pieces. Calculation failure means you stopped early.
Evaluation failure means you reached the right position and judged it wrongly. Each calls
for different practice.

Favor accuracy before speed, then add time limits. Guessing many puzzles can train
impulsiveness. Spending unlimited time on every easy pattern can also reduce practical
transfer. Use a deliberate pass followed by a timed pass.

Revisit failed positions after a delay. Solve from memory only if you can explain the
mechanism; remembering the first move alone is shallow. Change the position slightly and ask
whether the tactic still works.

Connect puzzles to your games. A motif missed in your own position carries emotional and
structural context, which can make the lesson durable. Build a small personal set of
recurring failures.
