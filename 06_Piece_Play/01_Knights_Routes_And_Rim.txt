Knights, routes, and outposts
=============================

Knights jump over occupied squares and attack in a fixed bent pattern. They are short-range
pieces, so their current square and future route matter greatly. A knight near the center
usually has more legal destinations, but mobility alone is not usefulness. An edge knight
that blockades a dangerous passer can be excellent.

Plan knight maneuvers backward from a destination. Identify an outpost or defensive square,
then find a route that does not abandon an urgent duty. A three-move journey is a promise to
give the opponent three turns. Check whether a pawn break, exchange, or attack will make the
destination obsolete before the knight arrives.

Stable knights thrive in closed structures. Pawns can protect them while enemy pawns cannot
chase them away. Long-range pieces may be unable to exchange them without surrendering
something valuable. In open positions, knights can struggle to influence both wings, though
a powerful central post can compensate for their limited range.

Knights are strong blockaders because they can stop a pawn while attacking around it. They
are also dangerous near a king because their attacks cannot be blocked. A knight check may
create a fork, and removing the knight may require a pawn move that weakens shelter.

The phrase 'a knight on the rim is dim' is a warning about reduced options, not a ban. A rim
maneuver may attack the bishop pair, support a pawn break, or route toward a stable post.
Ask where the knight is going and what it accomplishes on arrival. If the only answer is
'back to the center eventually,' the detour may be wasteful.

Knights have color-changing movement: each move takes them to a square of the opposite
color. This can help calculate routes and recognize that a knight cannot attack the same
target after one move if color makes that impossible. In endings, exact move counts matter
because a knight may need several turns to cross the board.

Against a knight, deny stable posts and create play on both wings. A bishop can change
direction more quickly. Do not push pawns merely to chase a harmless knight if those
advances create permanent weaknesses. Sometimes the correct response is to ignore it and
open the position elsewhere.

Exercise: select your worst knight from a quiet game. Give it a destination, a route, and a
useful job on arrival. Then identify the opponent's fastest way to invalidate the route. If
the route survives, you have a plan; if it fails, improve another piece or change the
structure first.
