Petroff, Philidor, Four Knights, and related development
========================================================

PETROFF DEFENSE

The Petroff answers the early knight attack symmetrically and challenges the first player's
center. Symmetry can produce equality, but copying moves blindly fails when the first player
uses the extra tempo. Central tactics occur early, so both sides must complete each exchange
before assuming a pawn is free.

Quiet branches lead to balanced structures where small development gains, open files, and
endgame technique matter. Aggressive branches test the center and king before castling.
Black's strategic aim is active equality, not passive imitation. White often seeks a lasting
initiative from space or piece activity rather than a direct material gain.

PHILIDOR DEFENSE

The Philidor supports the central pawn with another pawn and builds a compact position. The
cost is restricting a bishop and temporarily conceding space. Black must arrange a freeing
break or active piece placement; defending the center forever produces a cramped game.

White should develop and keep pressure without assuming that space wins by itself. Premature
sacrifices against the king can release Black's position. Lion and Hanham structures
describe compact arrangements with different move-order concerns. Understand which central
break Black seeks and which exchange would free the restricted bishop.

FOUR KNIGHTS GAME

Both sides develop both knights early, producing sound positions with many transpositions.
The apparent simplicity hides different centers. A bishop pin can lead toward Spanish
structures, an early central break toward the Scotch, and a quieter bishop development
toward symmetrical maneuvering.

Do not continue symmetry without purpose. The side moving first may break symmetry with a
central advance or active pin. The defender seeks exchanges or a timely break. Because
pieces are developed, central contact can occur without the same king danger as in
undeveloped gambits.

THREE KNIGHTS AND KING'S KNIGHT OPENINGS

These labels often describe early positions before a more specific family is reached. One
side may delay the second knight to preserve pawn options or choose another setup. Treat the
name as a junction. Ask which main family the structure can become and what the unusual
omission permits.

ELEPHANT AND LATVIAN GAMBITS

These countergambits strike aggressively before development is secure. They can create
traps, but accurate play often leaves the defender of the gambit accepting long-term risk. A
learner should study the central refutation ideas and calm development, not chase every
offered pawn. If using them, understand the objective cost and the positions after the
opponent avoids the trap.
