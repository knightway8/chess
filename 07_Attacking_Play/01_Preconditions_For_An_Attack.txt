Preconditions for a sound attack
================================

An attack needs access, force, and time. Access means lines or entry squares near the
target. Force means enough pieces can participate. Time means the opponent cannot create a
stronger threat or close the position before your pieces arrive. Missing one precondition
turns many attacks into gestures.

King safety is the most obvious target, but an attack can focus on a pawn, a weak square, a
trapped piece, or a promotion route. Define success before committing. If your pawn storm
cannot open a line and the opponent can counter in the center, the pawns may simply create
weaknesses.

Count attackers and defenders, then examine their quality. A pinned defender may be
unreliable. A knight far away may not arrive in time. A queen and bishop can be dangerous on
a diagonal, but one exchange may dissolve the battery. Raw numbers need geometric and
tactical context.

Central stability matters for wing attacks. When the center is closed, players often have
time to advance wing pawns and transfer pieces. When it can open, an uncastled or exposed
king may become vulnerable first. Before launching, ask what central break the opponent
gains and whether you can meet it.

Pawn advances near the king create hooks: pawns the opponent can attack to open files. You
can create a hook deliberately in the enemy shelter, but your own pawn storm may offer
counter-hooks. Calculate captures in both directions and identify which side benefits from
the opened line.

Bring the least active attacker. Moving the queen repeatedly may create threats, but a rook
or knight joining can make those threats durable. Attackers that arrive with tempo are
especially useful. Avoid placing every piece on the same line if one exchange can remove the
whole pressure.

The best defense may be a queen trade, a central break, or a counterattack. Test your plan
against all three. If the attack survives only passive defense, it is not yet convincing.
Sometimes the correct choice is to improve, restrict the counterplay, and launch one move
later.

Exercise: before a sacrifice near the king, write the accessible lines, participating
attackers, defender resources, and your result if the attack stops. If the final position
leaves you down material without lasting compensation, the burden of proof is high.
