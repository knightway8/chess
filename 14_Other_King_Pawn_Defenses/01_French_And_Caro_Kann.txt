French and Caro-Kann defenses
=============================

FRENCH DEFENSE

The French challenges the king-pawn center with a supported central pawn advance. White
often gains space and closes the center; Black attacks the pawn chain with a queenside break
and a later central break. Black's queen bishop can become restricted, making its
development a strategic problem. White must use space without allowing the center to
collapse.

The Advance Variation fixes the chain early. The Tarrasch uses the queen knight to support
the center without allowing a pin, accepting less natural placement. The Classical develops
the king knight and permits sharp central tension. The Winawer pins the queen knight and
accepts structural imbalance, often creating opposite-wing plans. Exchange lines open the
center and reduce space, but symmetrical pawns do not guarantee a draw.

Black's main question is how to free the queen bishop and attack the base or head of White's
chain. White's main question is whether to defend the center, advance it, or use the space
for an attack. Both sides should calculate when central exchanges open the king.

CARO-KANN DEFENSE

The Caro-Kann supports its central challenge with the queen-bishop pawn, usually allowing
the queen bishop to develop before the structure closes. Black aims for solidity with fewer
immediate piece restrictions than in the French. The support move costs a tempo, so White
can gain space or seek active development.

The Advance gains territory and leads to attacks on the pawn chain. The Classical and
related main lines resolve central tension and produce solid development. The Exchange
creates a characteristic structure with wing majorities and minority-attack ideas. The Panov
produces an isolated central pawn and active piece play. Fantasy systems support a large
center while taking on development and king-safety responsibilities.

Black should not confuse solidity with passivity. A central or queenside break is needed to
challenge White's space. White should not assume a small development lead proves an attack;
if Black completes development without concessions, the structure is durable.

COMPARISON

Both defenses challenge the first player's center from a supported base. The French accepts
a temporarily blocked queen bishop for immediate pressure. The Caro-Kann spends time
preparing the challenge so the bishop can often emerge. Learn the pawn-chain plans and
freeing breaks, then study exact tactics separately.
