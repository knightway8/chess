Scandinavian, Pirc, Modern, Alekhine, and Owen defenses
=======================================================

SCANDINAVIAN DEFENSE

The Scandinavian challenges White's central pawn immediately. After an exchange, Black often
develops the queen early and accepts attacks that gain White time. The benefit is a clear
structure and direct pressure. Black must choose queen placement carefully and complete
development; White should use tempi without overextending.

PIRC DEFENSE

The Pirc allows White to build a center, develops the king bishop on a long diagonal, and
later challenges the pawn mass. White can choose a broad attacking formation, quieter
development, or a positional setup. Black's breaks must be timed before space becomes a
bind. White's center is an asset only while supported.

MODERN DEFENSE

The Modern delays the king knight and keeps the pawn structure flexible while developing the
long-diagonal bishop. It can transpose to the Pirc or choose distinct setups. Flexibility
gives Black options but can concede development. White should occupy the center responsibly
and watch for attacks against its base.

ALEKHINE DEFENSE

The Alekhine invites White to advance central pawns while the king knight moves more than
once. Black aims to prove the pawn center overextended and attack it with pawn breaks and
pieces. White should develop behind the center rather than pushing every pawn. Black must
avoid being smothered before the challenge arrives.

OWEN DEFENSE

Owen's Defense develops the queen bishop on a long diagonal and attacks the center from the
side. Black keeps a flexible pawn structure but allows White space. A later central break is
essential. White should protect the center and develop with tempo where possible.

NIMZOWITSCH DEFENSE

This defense develops the queen knight against the king-pawn opening and invites several
transpositions. Black pressures the center without declaring a pawn structure. White can
occupy space or enter more familiar open-game formations. Both sides should examine what the
unusual order permits.

ST. GEORGE, BORG, AND HIPPOPOTAMUS SETUPS

These rare defenses use flank pawns or a compact back-rank arrangement before challenging
the center. They can be resilient and unfamiliar, but slow development gives White a chance
to occupy space. White should build without rushing a refutation. Black needs a coherent
break and must not remain permanently compressed.

The shared hypermodern lesson is invitation followed by attack. If Black never challenges
the center, the invitation becomes a concession. If White cannot maintain the center, the
gained space becomes a set of targets.
