Tilt, confidence, and objective decisions
=========================================

Tilt is a state in which emotion drives decisions after a surprise, mistake, or result.
Common signs are instant revenge attacks, refusal to defend, and excessive speed. Interrupt
the pattern by pausing, checking the current threats, and accepting that the previous
position no longer exists.

Confidence should support calculation, not replace it. An opponent's rating does not make
their move correct or your tactic impossible. Evaluate the board. Likewise, a weaker
opponent deserves the same search for best replies.

After a blunder, find the best remaining move. Do not sacrifice material simply to make the
game feel active. A worse position may have defensive resources; an emotional second mistake
can remove them.

Use a reset routine between games: record one lesson, leave detailed analysis for later, eat
or walk if appropriate, and begin the next game from zero. Carrying the last result into the
next opening distorts decisions.

Avoid self-labels such as 'bad at endings.' Identify a trainable behavior: missed
opposition, passive rook, or poor race counting. Specific faults can be practiced.

Objectivity includes practical context. Choosing a complicated line when you need a win can
be rational, but do not call it forced. Honest labels improve later review.
