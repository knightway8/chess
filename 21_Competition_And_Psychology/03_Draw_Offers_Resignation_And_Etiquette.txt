Draw offers, resignation, and etiquette
=======================================

A draw offer is a competitive decision governed by event rules and position judgment.
Consider the objective position, tournament situation, time, and realistic winning chances.
Do not use repeated offers to distract an opponent.

Resign when you judge that meaningful resistance is gone, but learners can benefit from
playing basic endings against appropriate opponents. Avoid forcing someone to demonstrate a
trivial mate solely from anger. In casual teaching games, expectations can be agreed in
advance.

Touch-move, clock, device, and claim procedures matter in tournaments. Learn the current
event rules before play and ask the arbiter when uncertain. Do not rely on an opponent's
interpretation during a dispute.

Good etiquette includes moving and pressing the clock consistently, avoiding distracting
behavior, and reporting results accurately. Respect does not require hiding competitive
intentions or accepting an unfavorable draw.

Online platforms implement draws, disconnections, assistance rules, and clocks differently.
Read their policies. Over-the-board FIDE rules are not automatically the interface behavior.

The board should decide the contest. Calm procedure protects both players and lets chess
skill remain central.
