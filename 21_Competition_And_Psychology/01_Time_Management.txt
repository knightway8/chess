Time management as a chess skill
================================

Spend time where the position changes. Irreversible pawn moves, tactical complications, and
transitions deserve attention. Forced recaptures and familiar developments usually deserve
less, unless a concrete surprise exists.

Keep a reserve. Reaching a winning position with no time to convert is incomplete time
management. Estimate how many decisions remain and protect enough time for them.

When the clock is low, simplify the thinking process: check threats, forcing moves, loose
pieces, and one safe candidate. Do not attempt a long positional speech. Prefer coordinated
moves that reduce immediate risk.

Avoid spending time trying to remember a line that is no longer on the board. Switch to
principles and calculate. Memory search can consume minutes without producing a legal
answer.

After games, note where time was spent and whether the decision justified it. A long think
that solved a critical position was useful even if the game was later lost. Repeated long
thinks over routine choices reveal a process problem.

Use the opponent's time to assess the position, but verify what changed after their move. Do
not lock in a reply before seeing its full effect.
