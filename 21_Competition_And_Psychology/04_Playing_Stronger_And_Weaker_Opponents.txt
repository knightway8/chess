Playing stronger and weaker opponents
=====================================

Play the position, not the rating. Against a stronger player, do not create unsound
complications merely because a normal game feels intimidating. Give them hard decisions
through sound active play.

Against a lower-rated player, do not force a win from an equal position. Maintain quality,
prevent counterplay, and accept that they can find good moves. Rating does not create a
tactical refutation.

Choose openings you understand. Surprise can be useful, but entering an inferior position to
avoid theory shifts the burden onto you. Familiar structures reduce cognitive load against
anyone.

If the opponent plays quickly, keep your own rhythm. Their speed may reflect preparation or
superficial thinking. Neither requires an instant reply. If they think for a long time, use
it to assess candidates without committing prematurely.

Tournament standings can influence risk, but define the result you need accurately. A
must-win situation may justify preserving complexity. A draw-secures-prize situation may
justify a sound simplification. The position still sets tactical limits.

Review without rating-based excuses. 'They were stronger' does not identify the error; 'I
exchanged into a lost pawn ending' does.
