Nimzo-Indian, Queen's Indian, and Bogo-Indian
=============================================

NIMZO-INDIAN DEFENSE

Black develops a bishop to pin the queen knight and increases control of the center without
immediately placing a pawn there. White must decide whether to accept doubled pawns, avoid
the structural change, or use the bishop pair. Black often gives up a bishop to damage
structure and control key squares.

The doubled pawns can be targets, but they may support central control and open a file.
White's bishop pair can become powerful if the position opens. Black seeks blockades and
pressure before that dynamic potential matures. Major branches such as the Classical,
Rubinstein, Sämisch, and Leningrad reflect different choices of queen development, modest
support, pawn expansion, and bishop activity.

QUEEN'S INDIAN DEFENSE

When White avoids the knight placement that permits the Nimzo pin, Black often develops the
queen bishop on a long diagonal and contests the center through piece pressure. White
chooses between building a pawn center, fianchettoing, or controlling key squares with
pieces. The struggle is strategic but contains tactics around the long diagonal.

Black wants active bishops and a timely central break. White can gain space but must prevent
the center from becoming fixed targets. Exchanges of the queen bishops change control of
light squares and often shape the middlegame.

BOGO-INDIAN DEFENSE

The Bogo uses an early bishop check when the Nimzo setup is unavailable. White can block
with a bishop, knight, or queen, each creating a different structure and development
pattern. Black often exchanges to achieve a sound position or retreats while maintaining
pressure.

The check is a development tool, not a promise to attack the king. Evaluate what blocks,
what recaptures, and whether Black has improved or surrendered the bishop without enough
gain.

CATALAN OPENING

The Catalan combines a queen-pawn center with a fianchettoed king bishop. White applies
long-term pressure along the diagonal and may offer the wing pawn temporarily. Black chooses
between holding the center, accepting the pawn and solving development, or creating a solid
closed setup.

White's compensation for a pawn can be lasting pressure, but recovery is not automatic.
Black should complete development and challenge the long bishop; White should avoid turning
positional pressure into an unjustified attack.

INDIAN DEFENSE AS A LABEL

The broad Indian Defense name includes early king-knight development against a queen-pawn
start before a specific structure is chosen. It is a junction, not a single strategy.
Determine which bishop development and central pawn break follow.
