Fortresses and the test of progress
===================================

A fortress is a position in which a material advantage cannot be converted because the
defender can maintain a stable setup and the attacker has no successful break or entry. It
is a property of the exact position, not a mood. Saying 'fortress' does not make one exist.

Test a fortress by listing the attacker's possible improvements. Can the king enter? Can a
pawn break change the structure? Can a piece be transferred to attack a new weakness? Can
the defender be placed in zugzwang? Can material be sacrificed to destroy the blockade? If
any route works, the fortress claim fails.

The defender wants a compact arrangement with independent coverage of key entry squares.
Avoid unnecessary pawn moves that create weaknesses or remove reserve tempi. Repeat the
stable setup when possible. Activity can still matter: checking from the correct side or
threatening a pawn may stop the attacker from reorganizing.

Material imbalance often creates fortress chances. A rook may be unable to defeat a bishop
and pawns if every entry is controlled. A queen can struggle against a protected structure
when checks never improve the position. These are possibilities, not universal results.
Placement and remaining pawns decide.

The attacker should be patient but purposeful. Move pieces to their best squares, create a
second target, and avoid exchanges that simplify into the defender's known setup. If the
position contains no progress, repeating moves may be an honest recognition of a draw rather
than a failure of will.

Zugzwang breaks many apparent fortresses. If every legal move weakens the setup, the
attacker can force entry by passing the turn at the right moment. Spare pawn moves can
determine who must move. This is why apparently irrelevant pawns matter in endings.

An engine evaluation alone may not explain practical progress. Analyze candidate plans and
verify whether the score changes under accurate defense. A stable numerical advantage can
coexist with a difficult conversion; a true fortress may receive misleading evaluations from
shallow analysis.

Exercise: for a claimed fortress, write the defender's repeating setup and the attacker's
four most plausible breaking ideas. Try them on a legal board. Keep the word fortress only
if every relevant idea fails.
